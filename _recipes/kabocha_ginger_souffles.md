## Source

What's for Dessert (Claire Saffitz), p. 317

## Notes

Serves 4. Four 6 oz ramekins, blender or food processor, hand mixer. Total ~2 hr 40 min (mostly roasting/cooling squash).

Kabocha base can be made several hours ahead (covered, room temp). Soufflés must be eaten straight from the oven. Don't double (won't bake evenly). Stand mixer works but whites overbeat faster.

Sub for kabocha: cooked sweet potato or red kuri squash.

Untested tweaks from [Chris Young's make-ahead soufflé](https://chrisyoungcooks.com/make-ahead-chocolate-souffle-recipe/) (scaled to 140g whites):
- Swiss meringue: whisk whites, sugar, and a pinch of salt over steam to 130°F (54°C), then whip to soft peaks (more stable, harder to overwhip).
- 0.7g (scant ¼ tsp) cream of tartar in the whites (0.5% of white weight).
- 0.15g xanthan gum (0.1% of white weight; easiest as 5g of a 3g xanthan + 97g sugar blend, taken out of the 50g sugar).
- Make ahead: fill ramekins ~1cm below the rim, chill 5 min, invert onto a sheet tray, refrigerate up to 48 hr. Flip and bake straight from the fridge (his chocolate version goes 375°F, ~20 min).

## Ingredients:

- {:.no-scale} 1 small kabocha squash (1.1kg / 2.5 lb), halved, seeds scooped out
- Butter and granulated sugar for the ramekins

### Kabocha base:

- 28g (2 tbsp) unsalted butter
- 120g (½ cup) pureed roasted kabocha
- 60g (¼ cup) whole milk
- 16g (2 tbsp) all-purpose flour
- 1.5g (½ tsp) Diamond Crystal kosher salt, plus a pinch
- 25g (2 tbsp) dark brown sugar
- 45g (3 large) egg yolks, divided
- ¾ tsp finely grated fresh ginger
- 4g (1 tsp) vanilla extract

### Whites:

- 140g (4 large) egg whites, room temp
- 50g (¼ cup) granulated sugar, plus more for topping

### To serve:

- Crème anglaise (p. 348, not transcribed)

## Instructions:

1. Rack in center, preheat oven to 425°F.
2. Roast squash cut-side down on a small sheet pan with a thin layer of water until completely tender, 50–60 min. Cool, scoop flesh, puree until smooth. Measure out 120g (save the rest).
3. Brush ramekins with room-temp butter using upward strokes all the way to the rim. Coat with granulated sugar, tap out excess.
4. Brown butter in a small saucepan over medium heat, stirring and scraping, until sputtering stops and golden specks form, ~2 min. Off heat, stir in kabocha. Whisk in milk, flour, and ½ tsp salt until smooth.
5. In a large bowl, whisk brown sugar and 2 yolks until pale and thick, ~1 min. Temper with a few tbsp of the hot kabocha mixture, 1 tbsp at a time (keep the bowl), then whisk it all back into the saucepan.
6. Cook over medium heat, whisking constantly, until thick like stiff pudding, ~4 min. Scrape into the reserved bowl and cool to room temp, whisking occasionally (or use an ice bath). Whisk in remaining yolk, ginger, and vanilla.
7. In a clean large bowl, beat whites and a pinch of salt on medium-low until frothy, ~20 sec. Medium-high until foamy and opaque, ~30 sec, then stream in sugar. Beat just to dense, glossy stiff peaks (don't overbeat).
8. Whisk ⅓ of the whites into the base, then fold in the rest in two additions until streak-free.
9. Fill ramekins to the very top, tap to settle, level flush with the rim using an offset spatula (discard any leftover; if short, fill as many as you can to the top).
10. Sprinkle tops with sugar. Run a thumb around the inside of each rim to wipe away a ring of batter (so they rise straight). Wipe the rims and outsides clean.
11. Put on a sheet pan, into the oven, and immediately drop to 375°F. Bake until risen, springy, with a slight wobble, 15–20 min.
12. Serve immediately with crème anglaise; spoon into the center and pour some in.
